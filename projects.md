## Selected Projects

### PhD projects: Competing Risks Data Analysis with Missing Biomarkers under Two-Phase Sampling

During my PhD, I developed statistical methods for the analysis of semiparametric cumulative incidence model 
with competing risks data when important biomarker information is available for only
a subset of study participants due to a two-phase sampling/Case-Cohort Design.

In biomedical research, measuring biomarkers can be expensive or
time-consuming. Researchers therefore often measure them in a selected
subset of participants using a two-phase sampling design. Accounting
for this selection is essential when estimating associations between
biomarkers and disease outcomes.

To address this issue, we developed inverse probability weighted estimation procedures 
for incomplete covariate data under generalized case-cohort/phase-two sampling designs.  
This method accounted for missingness by the designs and provided valid estimation and 
statistical inference with incomplete biomarker data. We also established the asymptotic 
properties of the proposed estimators and evaluated their finite-sample performance 
through simulation studies.

This methods were applied to the RV144 HIV vaccine trial to investigate associations
between immune-response biomarkers and the risk of infection with
different genetic types of HIV. This research was published in [*Computational Statistics & Data Analysis* (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5993453/).
